    Before you read the table

    The twelve terms that decide everything

    Tank vs. tankless. A tank unit slowly fills a 3–4 gallon pressure tank under the sink: water is ready instantly, it can push to a fridge line, and most need no electricity. A tankless unit makes water on demand with an electric pump — shoebox footprint, never-stale water, but nearly all need an outlet under the sink (the Frizzlife M800 is the one exception on this table) and fridge-line support varies — check the column, don't assume.
    GPD (gallons per day). Rated membrane output. Below ~25 GPD you can run a tank dry on a big cooking day; above that, any number is plenty for drinking. Tankless units need 400+ GPD to fill a glass quickly. Not a reason to pay more.
    Gallons drained per gallon made (lower is better). RO sends mineral-rich reject water down the drain. We converted every maker's ratio into this one number so it sorts and compares honestly: a pumped Home Master drains 1.0, a basic tank unit 3.0, a Waterdrop G3P800 0.33. The "$/yr" next to it is that water at Aquarion's ~1¢/gal for 3 gallons of drinking water a day — i.e. $4–45/yr. Real money only if you're on a septic or well pump.
    TDS (total dissolved solids). The one number that tells you RO is working. Our tap is ~200–300 ppm; good RO output is 5–30 ppm (90%+ rejection). A "TDS display" on the faucet shows it live; otherwise a $15 pen does the same job. Whirlpool's 86% rejection is the weakest here; everything else is 95–99%.
    Remineralization ("alkaline", "pH+", "remin" — same thing). RO water tastes flat because everything is stripped out. A remin stage dissolves 20–40 ppm of calcium/magnesium back in. It's about taste, not health: most families notice the flatness in plain water, few notice it in coffee. Built-in is simplest; "add-on" means a $30–60 extra cartridge.
    Stages. Marketing. "10-stage" often means six cartridges with sub-layers counted separately. Every system here has the three that matter: sediment/carbon prefilter → RO membrane → carbon polish. Ignore the number.
    NSF/ANSI certification — what the numbers mean. 58 = the assembled RO system was independently tested and works as claimed (lead, arsenic, TDS…). 53 = health contaminants; 401 = pharmaceuticals; P473 = PFOA/PFOS ("PFAS"); P231 = bacteria/virus; 372 = only means the metal parts are lead-free — it says nothing about filtering. "Components certified" (Home Master) means each cartridge and membrane carries a certification but nobody tested the assembled box — it works, it's just un-badged. "SGS-tested" (Frizzlife, SimPure) means the maker paid a commercial lab to run the NSF test method once; it's not the ongoing audited listing a real NSF mark is. Every RO membrane removes hardness and uranium (Aquarion's wells carry 2.3 ppb) regardless of badges.
    Needs outlet. Tankless pumps, electric booster pumps, UV lamps and instant-hot heaters need a GFCI outlet under the sink. Most kitchens have one for the disposal — look under the sink. If there isn't one, an electrician adds it for $150–350 (no permit for a single receptacle in Simsbury; a 1,200 W instant-hot heater should be on its own 20 A circuit, which is the upper end of that range). Tank units without a pump run on water pressure alone: no outlet, no electrician.
    Pumps. A permeate pump (Home Master "ERP") is a water-powered gadget that cuts waste to 1:1 — no electricity. A booster pump (iSpring "P", all tankless units) is electric.
    Fridge line. Whether the maker supports tee-ing the output to a refrigerator/ice maker. Tank units generally yes; tankless units are hit-or-miss (some need a $50–80 mini-tank). Running the line is +$75–200 of labor on any of them — it's the same ¼" tube whether the RO sits under the sink or in the basement.
    gpg / hardness. Grains per gallon; Aquarion Simsbury runs 10–16 gpg ("hard" to "very hard"). Hardness shortens RO membrane life — plan on the low end of any claim here, or add a $15–40 polyphosphate prefilter to protect it. See the main plan.
    Filter changes, leaks, noise. Every system here is a homeowner job: shut the feed valve, twist or pull cartridges, 10–20 minutes, once or twice a year. Twist-in heads (Aquasana, Whirlpool, Waterdrop, Frizzlife, Brondell) are tidier than the classic three-canister tank units (iSpring, APEC, Express) whose push-fit tubing is the usual leak point. Only the Culligan Smart has leak shutoff; a $10 water-alarm puck under the cabinet covers the rest. Tankless pumps hum at 40–50 dB (a quiet dishwasher) for 10–30 s per glass; tank units are silent.

    Cost columns, defined once: Unit = what you pay the store (faucet, tubing, drain saddle included on every consumer unit). Plumber-installed = unit + $200–450 labor (1.5–3 h) + $150–350 for an outlet when the unit needs power. Not included, because they're the same on every row: a new faucet hole if your sink has no spare sprayer/soap hole (+$50–150 stainless, +$150–300 granite), the fridge line (+$75–200), and the remin add-on cartridge on "add-on" units (+$30–60). True cost/yr = consumables + the membrane amortized over its rated life. Warranties: 1 year is standard for the online brands (3 with registration at iSpring/APEC); Home Master's 5 and Culligan's 10 are genuinely better.

    Fine print

    What we left out, and why

    • Countertop RO (Waterdrop K19 / K19-H, SimPure Y7, Bluevua ROPOT, AquaTru) — no plumbing, you refill a pitcher. Fine for one person; not an under-sink system.
    • Discontinued: Aquasana OptimH2O, AO Smith AO-US-RO-4000 (one letter-pair away from the current RO-MB-4000 in the table — check the box at Lowe's), GE GXRQ18NBN, Whirlpool WHER25, original Waterdrop G3 — still on some shelves, but no point buying into a dead filter line.
    • Commercial: iSpring RCS5T (500 GPD side-flow, no faucet) — a café machine.
    • Well-water variants of models above (Home Master TMULTRA-ERP / TMHP, iSpring RCC7AK-UV, APEC ROES-UV75) add UV lamps and iron filters. On chlorinated Aquarion water the UV does nothing but cost $35/yr in bulbs; buy the base model.
    • No-certification boutiques: Crystal Quest Thunder, Watts WP-4V, Everpure ROM IV (dealer, $1,400+) — nothing wrong with them, but we couldn't verify claims.
    • Bluevua makes no under-sink unit as of Aug 2026 despite old Amazon listings.
